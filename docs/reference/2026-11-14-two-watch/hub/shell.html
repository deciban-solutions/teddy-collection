If you could own only two watches

The Two-Watch Question

Six watch enthusiasts each picked the two watches they would keep if those two had to serve every day and every occasion. Three store staff ranked the pairs without knowing who picked them. Their reasons were brief, so these pages work out what they actually rewarded, and whether a collector's guidebook would agree.

The short answer

The judges wanted two everyday watches, not one daily and one special. Ranking by how many of the two watches are rated to 100 m or more, then by the simpler pair, reproduces their exact order: Brit, Adrien, Teddy, Marco, James, Andrew.
Their talk of “spice” did not decide it. The most colourful, complicated pair came third; one panelist said on the spot, “They want spice but chose this.”
A collector's guidebook would crown a different pair. Blind scorers using collection-building criteria put James's dress GMT and dive watch first, the pair the judges put fifth, because together they cover the widest range of occasions.
Everyone picked true to their own taste. The winners were the two whose personal taste was most conventional, which is what a blind judge has to assume about the wearer.
